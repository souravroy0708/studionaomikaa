Planning a Small Apartment So It Never Feels Small

Most small apartments don't feel small because of their square footage — they feel small because of decisions made before anyone picked a paint colour: where a wall sits, which way a door swings, and how much of the floor gets swallowed by things nobody uses every day.

After a few hundred residential walkthroughs, three habits consistently separate a compact home that feels tight from one that feels generous.

1. Protect one long sightline

Every apartment has at least one direction you can see furthest — often from the entry through to a window. Keeping that line clear of furniture, tall storage or half-walls does more for the sense of space than almost any other single decision. It costs nothing and it's the first thing we check on a site visit.

A room reads as big or small based on the longest uninterrupted line your eye can travel, not the total floor area.

2. Give storage a discipline, not just a location

Small homes fail when storage is added reactively — a shelf here, a cabinet there — until surfaces disappear under things with nowhere else to go. We plan storage by category before choosing furniture: where shoes live, where documents live, where seasonal items live. A wardrobe sized correctly for what you actually own beats a bigger one filled with overflow.

3. Let light travel between rooms, not just into them

Internal glazing, tall doors, and light-toned surfaces near doorways let daylight carry from a bright room into a dimmer one next to it. This one change — treating light as something that moves through a home rather than just entering it — is often the difference between rooms that feel connected and ones that feel like separate boxes.

None of this requires more space

Every one of these habits is a planning decision, not a renovation budget line. That's the core of how we approach residential interiors at Studio Naomikaa: get the plan right first, and the finishes will do far less work than people expect.
